Reliable robotic manipulation

Independent research · 2026–present

A nonlinear 2-DOF MATLAB/Simulink platform for testing torque-aware PID and Fuzzy-PID control, trajectory execution, and robustness. The latest study asks whether fixed-controller results survive finer simulation and feedback time steps.

On 112 held-out requested simulation tasks, reliable success rose from 17.9% (20/112) with the original Fuzzy-PID to 50.9% (57/112) with the research controller. In a separate three-case numerical study, mean paired joint RMS tracking error fell 90.7% versus the initial manually tuned PID.

Study scope

Simulation, Adaptive Control, and Optimization Framework for Reliable Robotic Manipulation. The 112-task success comparison comes from an earlier held-out validation. The later numerical study used five prespecified cases and four fixed controllers. All 40 comparisons were complete; six changed beyond a preregistered tolerance when both plant and controller ran at 0.5 ms. Controllers were not retuned, and no confirmation set was opened. Results are simulation-only.

Robot mount & trajectory evaluation

Ragtime Lab · Motion & evaluation

A pipeline for testing how recorded dual-hand tasks map to PiperX installations, using strict inverse kinematics, collision checks, and the original controller-event timing.

This full 17.64-second Fold Box simulation follows 97.88% of paired raw-tool targets within 1 mm and 0.5° after a mount adjustment, up from 94.83%. Failed frames show a safe hold; the simulated state, swept-edge, and topology checks report zero violations.

Methods & scope

The broader evaluation covered 27 trajectories from 12 task families and 4 mount layouts. This video shows one complete optimized recording, with no retiming or object model. Nine target frames are geometrically incompatible with the 15 mm clearance rule even at the stated tolerances, setting a 98.81% upper bound for this raw recording. It is a MuJoCo kinematic playback of the saved joint states, not forward-dynamics or hardware execution. The published joint path exceeds the PiperX speed and acceleration limits and is not a hardware-ready command.

About me

I’m Zhenghao YU, a Computer Engineering undergraduate at The University of Hong Kong . My work spans robot design and control, autonomous driving, embedded systems, and human-computer interaction. Across these areas, I focus on how systems behave beyond ideal test conditions.

At Ragtime Lab, I curated multimodal factory data and evaluated robot designs, mounting, and trajectories. Separately, I study reliable manipulation through nonlinear simulation, controller tuning, and numerical sensitivity tests. Earlier projects took me from adversarial highway-merging tests to wheel-leg navigation and contactless vehicle interfaces.

I want my work to hold up when others look closely: data they can inspect, experiments they can repeat, and results that show where a system works, and where it doesn’t.

Research interests

Robot Design and Evaluation

Comparing robot designs and mount layouts against task requirements, then checking motion feasibility, collisions, and execution in simulation.

Trustworthy Autonomous Driving

Finding failure cases in highway-merging planners through adversarial scenarios, multi-agent learning, and SOTIF-aligned testing.

Robotic Systems and Navigation

Connecting sensor fusion, global and local planning, and balance control on wheel-leg robotic platforms.

Human-Centered Embodied Interaction

Designing and evaluating gaze, head, and voice interaction for vehicle cabins where noise and vibration limit conventional interfaces.
